Microbial Population Dynamics

Mathematical Models of Bacterial Growth

1. Exponential Growth (Unrestricted Phase)

When a bacterial colony is inoculated into a fresh, nutrient-rich medium with unlimited resources, it enters the logarithmic (log) phase. During this period, cells undergo rapid binary fission. Because there are no environmental constraints, the growth rate is strictly proportional to the current population.

P(t) = P_0 \cdot e^{kt}

2. The Logistic Equation (Resource Depletion)

In reality, exponential growth cannot continue indefinitely. As the colony expands, it inevitably faces nutrient depletion, space limitations, and the accumulation of toxic metabolic waste. Growth begins to slow, eventually reaching a state of colony saturation known as the stationary phase. The population stabilizes at the environment's maximum capacity.

P(t) = \frac{M}{1 + K e^{-kt}}
